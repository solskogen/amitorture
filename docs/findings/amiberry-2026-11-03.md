# Amiberry findings, amitorture (2026-11-03)

**Tested binaries:**
- master `3f6d592ab` (sha256 `c5de705d…`) until 17:00
- then master `435db0509` (sha256 `d348a69b…`), which includes #2592-#2597 (WinUAE CPU/FPU/JIT, chipset/display, core, host and vendored merges)

aarch64 host (Raspberry Pi 500). The "master" column below is the status on `435db0509`.

**Confidence:**
- **confirmed:** cause read in the source, matches the test exactly
- **likely:** test result clear, code location plausible, cause not pinned
- **check-on-hw:** the manual says X, but Amiberry/WinUAE may model a real-chip quirk. Confirm on real hardware or FPGA first.
- **by design:** a deliberate accuracy/speed trade-off; listed so nobody chases it

**Fixes (2026-11-04):** a local Amiberry branch `amitorture-fixes` (on master `bcea436dd`, not merged) fixes H1, H2, S1, S2, S8, S9, F7, the 12-hour part of R2, and part of V4; one commit per finding, each verified with amitorture: the targeted cases before/after, and a 3-run smoke `check` against a 3-run baseline of unmodified master with no regressions. The xfail entries stay until the fixes are in master: on the branch they show as `fixed`.

**Noise floor (fast cores, 4 parallel jobs):** two baselines of the same binary differ in some display cases on the fast cores (mode_hires_2bpl, mode_lores_*bpl, clx_spr_*, scroll_pf*: fail in 1-2 of 3 runs), devio.td_u1_rw255_w2 on the cycle-exact A500, misc_hw.rtc_rate, and most host.speed_* (untestable when the host is busy). Judge fixes on 3+ runs, and against a baseline of the same host.

Every row names the amitorture case(s). Runner reports (`work/results/*/REPORT.md`) carry the inputs, the expected and actual values, the manual rule, and a repro pack. Known findings are registered in `runner/amitorture/xfail.json`, so they show as `xfail` and their fixes as `fixed`.

Triage of the final run (`work/results/20261103-215821Z`). Verified with `disk/build-triage/set` (core, chip and exp disks) on 9 smoke entries, 4 emulators in parallel: a1200 fast ± JIT, a4000 040/060 fast ± JIT, AROS, a600 compat NTSC and a500 CE, plus an a1200 cycle-exact run for the display stress:

- **paula.wordsync_waits (was P1; now on a4000 040/060 fast ± JIT and AROS):** test-side. `dsk_dma()` leaves ADKCON at $7700 (FAST off) between transfers, so Paula samples the DD disk under DF0's head with 4 µs cells. MFM gaps and runs of $00/$FF data put a pulse in every 4 µs cell and shift in as all ones, so the shift register can hold $FFFF. Amiberry models this too (disk.cpp `getonebit`, `nextbit()` = 2 without FAST). The next transfer set FAST, then DSKSYNC=$FFFF and DSKLEN within a few µs. `DSKSYNC()` matched the stale register (raised DSKSYN, which the test then cleared), and the next 1 bit kept it at $FFFF, so `wordsync_detected()` started the DMA with no second DSKSYN. That matches the record: dskblk=1, dsksyn=0, DSKBYTR WORDEQUAL seen, buffer $5555. It became frequent because the new chip disk has long zero runs on track 0. Fix: wait ~110 µs (56 cells) after the ADKCON write, before DSKSYNC/DSKLEN. The old disk still fails on a4000-68040nommu-fast with the same signature; the fixed one passes on all 6 entries.
- **cia.ciab_tod_counts_hsync (part of H3, 6 fast/JIT entries):** test-side. The (frame, line) stamp added one frame when INTREQ VERTB was pending at line < 100. Amiberry sets VERTB a few colour clocks after V wraps to 0: a captured sample read V=0 H=2 with VERTB still clear, so the stamp dropped by a whole frame. `tod_wait()` then ended early (unsigned compare), and `got.delta` was 88/98/193 with negative window bounds. Fix: the stamps also follow the beam (never go back; a drop of V within the same frame number counts as a wrap). The HRM gives no exact VERTB position.
- **cia.ciab_tod_write_lo_starts (rest of H3):** undefined. The TOD advanced one more than the stamp window allows: 12 in a 10-line window, maximum 11. Amiberry applies the current line's already-received HSYNC at the restart (cia.cpp `CIA_tod_check`, called after `todon = 1`; in non-cycle-accurate mode immediately). The 8520 data sheet doesn't say whether a pulse in flight across the TODLO write counts. The case now allows +1 on the maximum and says why.
- **cia.ciaa_tb_counts_ta_underflows (AROS; also a1200-fast-thr earlier, both CIAs, always exactly one short):** test-side, not AROS-specific. TB was read right after the CRA read that saw TA's START clear. Amiberry's non-cycle-accurate CIA passes the TA underflow to TB through a 2-E-clock input pipe (cia.cpp `process_pipe`), and a fast emulated CPU can do both reads within one E clock. Real CIA accesses are each synchronised to their own E cycle, and no cascade latency is specified. Fix: wait 8 E clocks before reading TB.
- **misc_hw_os.zorro_memlist (AROS only):** test-side. The case already meant to skip on AROS, whose Zorro III RAM board (2011/83 at $40000000) is configured but not in exec's MemList when the bootblock runs (cd_Flags $02; Kickstart 3.1 links the same board, cd_Flags $0A). - **timing.lines_per_frame_lace/_nolace (68000 compat/CE, PAL and NTSC; JIT "stuck"):** test-side. The frame length was "highest V seen + 1". On a 68000 one poll iteration took ~170 cck (0.75 line), longer at the guard. Measured: a frame missed its last line in most runs of 8 on a500-68000-ce and a600-68000-compat-ntsc. Non-cycle-exact cores can also skip several lines (a 305-line frame on a1200 fast JIT threaded). The HRM doesn't bound CPU speed against the beam. The length now comes from copper marks: WAIT line 311/261 sets INTREQ DSKBLK, WAIT 312/262 sets SOFT, both read at each V wrap. That is the method display.beam_frame_lines uses; it passes everywhere. after the change 48/48 case results passed (6 repetitions × 2 cases on a500 CE, a600 compat NTSC and two JIT entries).
- **timing.eclock_cck_ratio / lines_per_frame_* "beam stuck" on JIT twins:** test-side. Besides the E-clock bound, the poll guard gave up after 4M polls with neither the beam nor the CIA moving. Amiberry's non-cycle-exact cores keep only the CPU running while they wait for the host's frame time (events.cpp `event_check_vsync`: "Keep only CPU emulation running while waiting for sync point", is_syncline -10/-11), so a JIT can legitimately see both clocks frozen for millions of polls. The bound is now 32M polls (68000/010) or 512M (020+), also for cck_per_line's sample blocks, and a stuck record names the bound that expired (`timing.bound`). That diagnostic then showed a second cause: "E-clock limit" with negative `e_ticks` on both JIT twins. The 32-bit E counter is CIA-B TB counting TA underflows; a fast CPU read TB in the 2-E-clock window before TB counted the TA reload ($FFFF), got a value 65536 too high, and the unsigned elapsed-time check expired at once. `ec_read()` now re-reads while TA is within 16 E clocks of the reload, and the guard ignores a counter that reads back above its start. The ratio itself now takes the CPU's wrap-detection delay off (beam position read next to the E counter; `timing.lead0/1_cck_x2`, ~900-1400 half-cck, i.e. 2-3 lines, on the 68000). Before, a late detection at one end counted as E-clock error: a1200-fast-thr, diff 1830 > tol 1621 in run 160545Z.

## Config facts worth knowing (not bugs)
- **The JIT needs `cycle_exact=false` and `cpu_compatible=false`.** Otherwise it is silently off. Option order matters: `cycle_exact=true` is dropped if a nonzero `cachesize` came first.
- **`mmu_model` is silently dropped with the JIT** (A4000 preset): MMU configs need `cachesize=0`.
- **Plain hardfiles on IDE/SCSI get a virtual RDB** prepended.
- **CD32 and CDTV hold INT2 asserted** (Akiko / CD controller), as real hardware does.
- **Amiberry IPC has no joystick, CD32-pad or PCMCIA injection;** those amitorture cases stay `untestable`.
- **`rtc=` is ignored on every preset machine** (any `chipset_compatible` other than Generic). `fixup_prefs` (main.cpp ~459-466) reruns `built_in_chipset_prefs`, which sets `cs_rtc = 0` (cfgfile.cpp ~10825) and then only the preset's clock: the A500 gets an MSM6242B only with slow RAM at $C00000, the A500+, A1200 (with fast RAM), CD32, CDTV, A3000 and A4000 their built-in one. The user's `cs_ide` and `cs_mbdmac` are saved and restored around that call, `cs_rtc` is not; `--dump-config` still shows the requested `rtc=`, so the change is invisible to config checks. The matrix entries `a500-rtc-a501` and `a500plus-rtc` are therefore the same machines as the A500 (with slow RAM) and A500+ presets. This is intended: the GUI's `rtc_of()` (osdep/settings/settings_chipset.cpp) mirrors the preset table and offers only the clock the preset forces. To test an RTC choice, use `chipset_compatible=-` (Custom).
